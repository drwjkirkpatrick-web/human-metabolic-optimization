# 20-Week Pharmacogenomics Learning Plan

A structured curriculum for mastering the most clinically relevant pharmacogenomic variants, from critical safety variants to advanced functional SNPs.

---

## Course Overview

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Duration** | 20 weeks (12 core + 8 advanced) |
| **Commitment** | 3-5 hours/week |
| **Prerequisites** | Basic genetics, pharmacology fundamentals |
| **Outcome** | Competency in interpreting Tier 1-3 PGx variants + functional SNPs |
| **Assessment** | Weekly quizzes + Midterm + Final comprehensive examination |

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. **Interpret** CPIC guideline recommendations for major pharmacogenes
2. **Identify** high-risk genotypes requiring immediate clinical action
3. **Apply** genotype-guided dosing adjustments
4. **Recognize** population-specific variant frequencies
5. **Counsel** patients on pharmacogenomic test results
6. **Navigate** PharmGKB and CPIC resources independently

### **WEEK 1: Foundations of Pharmacogenomics**

**Topics:**
- History and scope of pharmacogenomics
- CPIC evidence levels (1A, 1B, 2A, 2B)
- Gene nomenclature: star alleles vs. rs numbers
- Basic mechanisms: CYP450 enzymes, phase I/II metabolism

**Key Resources:**
- CPIC website overview: https://cpicpgx.org/
- PharmGKB tutorial: https://www.pharmgkb.org/page/tutorials

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Read CPIC "About" page and evidence definitions
- [ ] Watch PharmGKB introductory video (30 min)
- [ ] Review gene nomenclature (CYP2D6 *1 vs. *4 vs. *10)
- [ ] Complete Week 1 quiz (10 questions)

**Clinical Pearl:** 
> "Not all genetic variants are created equal. A 'mutation' in a pharmacogene may be clinically silent or life-threatening depending on the drug. Always check CPIC level before making clinical decisions."

### **WEEK 2: Tier 1 - DPYD (Dihydropyrimidine Dehydrogenase)**

**Topics:**
- 5-FU/capecitabine metabolism pathway
- DPYD *2A, *13, HapB3, c.2846A>T variants
- Clinical presentation of DPD deficiency
- CPIC dosing recommendations

**Key Variants to Master:**
| Variant | rs Number | Action |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| DPYD *2A | rs3918290 | Contraindicate 5-FU |
| DPYD *13 | rs55886062 | 50% dose reduction |
| HapB3 | rs75017182 | 50% dose reduction |

**Case Study:**
> A 58-year-old with colon cancer is prescribed FOLFOX. DPYD testing shows *2A heterozygosity. What do you recommend?

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Read CPIC DPYD guideline (full text)
- [ ] Memorize the 4 key DPYD variants
- [ ] Calculate dose reductions for different genotypes
- [ ] Complete case study analysis

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "DPYD *2A is the most dangerous variant in oncology. Never give 5-FU to a homozygous carrier. Heterozygotes need significant dose reduction or alternative therapy."

### **WEEK 3: Tier 1 - TPMT (Thiopurine Methyltransferase)**

**Topics:**
- Thiopurine metabolism (6-MP, 6-TG, azathioprine)
- Star allele nomenclature (*2, *3A, *3C)
- Phenotype assignment (poor, intermediate, normal)
- Dose adjustment calculations

**Key Variants to Master:**
| Allele | rs Numbers | Function |
|--------|------------|----------|
| *2 | rs1800462 | No function |
| *3A | rs1800460 + rs1142345 | No function |
| *3C | rs1142345 | Reduced function |

**Dosing Quick Reference:**
| Phenotype | Genotype Example | 6-MP Starting Dose |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| Poor | *2/*3A | Avoid OR 10% |
| Intermediate | *1/*3A | 30-70% |
| Normal | *1/*1 | 100% |

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Memorize TPMT star alleles and their effects
- [ ] Practice phenotype assignment from genotype
- [ ] Calculate pediatric ALL dosing scenarios
- [ ] Complete Week 3 case: IBD patient starting azathioprine

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "TPMT testing is now standard of care before starting thiopurines. One TPMT *3A homozygous patient given standard 6-MP dosing can develop fatal pancytopenia within days."

**Key Tests:**

|------------|------|------------|--------|
| B*57:01 | Abacavir | All | Contraindicate if positive |

**Study Activities:**

**Clinical Pearl:**

### **WEEK 5: Tier 2 - CYP2C19 (Clopidogrel & PPIs)**

**Topics:**

**Key Variants to Master:**
| Allele | rs Number | Effect | Frequency (Caucasian) |
|--------|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| *2 | rs4244285 | No function | ~15% |
| *3 | rs4986893 | No function | ~0.5% |
| *17 | rs12248560 | Increased | ~20% |

**Clinical Scenarios:**
1. Post-stent patient: CYP2C19 *2/*2 → Use ticagrelor
2. GERD patient: CYP2C19 *17/*17 → Standard PPI okay
3. Post-MI patient: CYP2C19 *1/*2 → May need higher clopidogrel dose

**Study Activities:**

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "15-30% of Asians are CYP2C19 poor metabolizers. This explains why East Asian post-PCI patients have higher stent thrombosis rates on clopidogrel. Always consider genotype or use alternative agents."

### **WEEK 6: Tier 2 - CYP2D6 (Opioids & Psychotropics)**

**Topics:**
- CYP2D6 substrate categories
- Activity score calculation
- Codeine and tramadol black box warnings
- Tamoxifen metabolism

**Key Variants to Master:**
| Phenotype | Activity Score | Codeine | Tramadol |
|-----------|---------------|---------|----------|
| Poor | 0 | **AVOID** | Reduce dose |
| Intermediate | 0.5 | Avoid | Reduce |
| Normal | 1.0-2.0 | Standard | Standard |
| Ultra-rapid | >2.0 | **AVOID** | Caution |

**Star Alleles:**
- **No function:** *3, *4, *5, *6
- **Reduced:** *9, *10, *17, *41
- **Normal:** *1, *2
- **Increased:** *1xN, *2xN (duplications)

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Calculate activity scores for 10 genotypes
- [ ] Practice phenotype assignment
- [ ] Review FDA codeine warnings
- [ ] Case: Postpartum pain management in breastfeeding mother

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "Codeine deaths in children led to FDA contraindication in post-tonsillectomy patients. If you remember one thing: NEVER give codeine to CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolizers, especially children."

### **WEEK 7: Tier 2 - CYP2C9/VKORC1 (Warfarin)**

**Topics:**
- Warfarin metabolism and mechanism
- CYP2C9 *2 and *3 variants
- VKORC1 -1639G>A
- Warfarin dosing algorithms

**Key Variants:**
| Gene | Variant | rs Number | Effect |
|------|---------|-----------|--------|
| CYP2C9 | *2 | rs1799853 | 30% dose reduction |
| CYP2C9 | *3 | rs1057910 | 50% dose reduction |
| VKORC1 | -1639G>A | rs9923231 | 25-50% dose reduction |

**Combined Effect:**
- *1/*1 + GG = Standard dose (~5-7 mg)
- *2/*3 + AA = 0.5-2 mg starting dose

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Use warfarin dosing calculator with genotype
- [ ] Calculate expected doses for 5 patient scenarios

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "Warfarin pharmacogenomics is helpful but not mandatory. The rise of DOACs (which don't require monitoring) has reduced the importance of warfarin PGx, but it's still valuable for mechanical valves and severe renal impairment."

**Drugs to Avoid in G6PD Deficiency:**

- Dapsone

- Methylene blue

**UGT1A1:**
| Genotype | TA Repeat | Irinotecan Action |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| *1/*1 | 6/6 | Standard dose |
| *1/*28 | 6/7 | Reduce dose |
| *28/*28 | 7/7 | Avoid or extreme reduction |

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] List all oxidant drugs contraindicated in G6PD
- [ ] Calculate hemolytic risk by variant class
- [ ] Review irinotecan FOLFIRI regimen dosing
- [ ] Case: Malaria treatment in G6PD-deficient traveler

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "G6PD testing is mandatory before primaquine for malaria. One dose of primaquine in a Mediterranean variant patient can cause massive hemolysis and acute kidney injury."

## **WEEK 13: MTHFR Deep Dive — Methylation Mastery**

**Topics:**
- One-carbon metabolism biochemistry review
- MTHFR C677T and A1298C functional mechanisms
- Compound heterozygote interpretation
- MTHFR rs2274976 and rs3737965 (population-specific)
- Methylation vs. transsulfuration balance

### Key Variants to Master
| SNP | cDNA/Protein | Effect | Clinical Impact |
|-----|--------------|--------|-----------------|
| **C677T** | c.665C>T p.Ala222Val | Thermolabile; ~70% activity in TT | Homocysteine elevation; folate responsive |
| **A1298C** | c.1298A>C p.Glu429Ala | ~60% activity; BH4 recycling | Purine synthesis; compound het risk |
| **rs2274976** | c.1296C>T p.Gly429= | mRNA stability | Asian populations; NTD risk |
| **rs3737965** | 5'UTR | Transcriptional | Promoter regulation |

### MTHFR Genotype Interpretation Practice
| C677T | A1298C | Expected Activity | Clinical Action |
|-------|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| CC | AA | 100% | Standard folate |
| CT | AA | ~70% | Monitor homocysteine |
| TT | AA | ~30-40% | L-methylfolate preferred |
| CT | AC | ~50-60% | Compound het = higher risk |
| CC | CC | ~60% | BH4 support if needed |

### Clinical Applications
1. **Cardiovascular**: Homocysteine >15 μmol/L + MTHFR variant
2. **Neural Tube Defects**: Preconception counseling
3. **Depression/Anxiety**: Methylfolate augmentation (Deplin)
4. **Fertility**: Implantation failure associations

**Case Study:**
> A 32-year-old female planning pregnancy has MTHFR C677T/A1298C compound heterozygote. Homocysteine is 18 μmol/L. Design a preconception protocol.

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Calculate enzyme activity for 6 genotype combinations
- [ ] Compare folic acid vs. L-methylfolate metabolism
- [ ] Review homocysteine reference ranges by lab
- [ ] Analyze 3 preconception case studies

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "MTHFR C677T is the most over-tested yet under-understood variant. While it affects homocysteine, the clinical impact is often modest. Don't over-interpret—always check actual homocysteine levels and response to treatment."

## **WEEK 14: CBS — The Transsulfuration Pathway**

**Topics:**
- Homocysteine → cystathionine → cysteine → glutathione
- CBS 844ins68 protective mechanism
- CBS C699T and T833C functional effects
- Transsulfuration vs. remethylation balance
- B6 responsiveness

### Key Variants
| Variant | rs Number | cDNA/Protein | Effect | Clinical |
|---------|-----------|--------------|--------|----------|
| **844ins68** | — | 68bp insertion in exon 8 | Generally protective; ↓ homocysteine | Common; no action needed |
| **C699T** | — | c.699C>T p.Thr236= | Improved splicing; ↓ homocysteine | Protective |
| **T833C** | — | c.833T>C p.Ile278Thr | Reduced enzyme activity | ↑ Homocysteine; B6 responsive |
| **rs5742905** | rs5742905 | Promoter | ↓ transcription | Variable; monitor |

### Homocysteine Management by CBS Variant
| CBS Status | Homocysteine Effect | B6 Dose | Monitoring |
|------------|---------------------|---------|------------|
| **844ins68 carrier** | ↓ 10-20% lower | Standard | Less frequent |
| **C699T carrier** | ↓ 5-15% lower | Standard | Less frequent |
| **T833C carrier** | ↑ 15-30% higher | 50-100mg B6 | Monthly initially |
| **rs5742905 variant** | Variable | 25-50mg B6 | Bi-monthly |

### Transsulfuration Pathway Integration
```
Homocysteine ──CBS/B6──→ Cystathionine ──CSE──→ Cysteine
                              ↓
                        Glutathione (antioxidant)
                              ↓
                        Taurine or sulfate
```

**Clinical Applications:**
1. **Ammonia detoxification**: CBS upregulation pathway
2. **Glutathione synthesis**: Cysteine availability
3. **Sulfur metabolism**: Sulfate for proteoglycans
4. **Oxidative stress**: GSH recycling

**Case Study:**
> A patient with homocysteine of 22 μmol/L is compound heterozygous for MTHFR C677T and CBS T833C. What's your prioritized treatment approach?

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Draw transsulfuration pathway with enzymes
- [ ] Calculate B6 doses for different CBS variants
- [ ] Review glutathione synthesis biochemistry
- [ ] Compare CBS vs. MTR/MTRR contribution to homocysteine

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "CBS 844ins68 is one of the few truly protective variants—it actually improves homocysteine clearance. Don't treat carriers differently unless homocysteine is elevated for other reasons."

## **WEEK 15: MTR/MTRR — The B12 Connection**

**Topics:**
- Methionine synthase (MTR) and reductase (MTRR) function
- B12 trafficking and functional B12 deficiency
- MTR A2756G and c.3518C>T variants
- MTRR A66G, K3504R, and rs2287780
- Distinguishing serum B12 from functional B12 status

### Key Variants
| Gene | SNP | cDNA/Protein | Effect | Clinical |
|------|-----|--------------|--------|----------|
| **MTR** | A2756G | c.2756A>G p.Asp919Gly | ↓ remethylation efficiency | ↑ Homocysteine; neuropathy |
| **MTR** | c.3518C>T | c.3518C>T p.Ser1173Leu | Reduced activity | Less common; B12 responsive |
| **MTRR** | A66G | c.66A>G p.Ile22Met | Impaired MTR reactivation | Functional B12 deficiency |
| **MTRR** | K3504R | c.1517A>G p.Lys506Arg | Reduced reductase activity | Homocysteine elevation |
| **MTRR** | rs2287780 | Intronic | Splicing regulation | Variable expression |

### Functional B12 Deficiency Pattern
| Marker | True B12 Deficiency | Functional B12 Deficiency (MTR/MTRR) |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Serum B12 | Low (<200 pg/mL) | Normal (200-900 pg/mL) |
| Homocysteine | Elevated | Elevated |
| Methylmalonic acid (MMA) | Elevated | Normal |
| MCV | High | Normal or high |
| Response to oral B12 | Good | May need methylcobalamin |

### Clinical Applications
1. **Neuropathy**: Methyl-B12 preferred with MTR variants
2. **Cognitive decline**: Functional B12 deficiency screening
3. **Pregnancy**: NTD prevention beyond folate
4. **Methylation support**: B12 form selection

**Case Study:**
> A 58-year-old presents with numbness/tingling. Serum B12 is 450 pg/mL (normal), homocysteine 19 μmol/L, MMA 120 nmol/L (normal). MTR A2756G and MTRR A66G positive. What's the diagnosis and treatment?

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Differentiate true vs. functional B12 deficiency
- [ ] Compare cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, hydroxocobalamin
- [ ] Calculate B12 dosing by variant combination
- [ ] Review B12 absorption vs. utilization pathways

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "MTR/MTRR variants can cause functional B12 deficiency with completely normal serum B12 levels. If homocysteine is high but MMA is normal, think functional deficiency—and use methylcobalamin, not cyanocobalamin."

|-----------|--------|--------|--------|--------|----------|-------------------|
| **HPS** (High Pain Sensitivity) | G | C | C | Met | Lowest | ↑ Pain; ↓ stress resilience; better executive function; ↑ opioid need |
| **LPS** (Low Pain Sensitivity) | A | T | T | Val | Highest | ↓ Pain; ↑ stress resilience; ADHD risk; ↓ opioid need |
| **APS** (Average) | Mixed | Mixed | Mixed | Val/Met | Intermediate | Standard responses |

### Clinical Applications by Domain

**Pain Management:**
- HPS haplotype = 30-50% higher opioid requirements
- LPS haplotype = better pain tolerance; lower opioid need
- Consider haplotype testing before chronic opioid therapy

**Psychiatry:**
- Met/Met (low activity) = better prefrontal cognition, but stress-sensitive
- Val/Val (high activity) = more resilient, but may need higher stimulant doses
- COMT × DRD2 interactions in antipsychotic response

**Estrogen Metabolism:**
- COMT methylates catechol estrogens (2-OH-E1, 4-OH-E1)
- Low COMT = higher catechol estrogen levels
- Potential breast cancer risk implications

**Case Study:**
> A patient with fibromyalgia has HPS haplotype (Met/Met at rs4680). Currently on 30mg oxycodone daily with inadequate pain control. How do you adjust?

**Study Activities:**
- [ ] Determine haplotype from 4-SNP genotype data
- [ ] Calculate pain sensitivity predictions
- [ ] Compare COMT effects in men vs. women (estrogen interactions)
- [ ] Review COMT × OPRM1 (opioid receptor) interactions

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "COMT Val158Met alone explains only ~10% of pain variability. The full HPS/LPS/APS haplotype is more predictive. Remember—COMT is about dopamine clearance, which affects pain modulation, reward, and executive function together."

**Topics:**
- MAOA vs. MAOB substrate specificity

- rs1799836 and MAOB activity

### Key Variants
| Gene | Variant | cDNA/Protein | Effect | Clinical |
|------|---------|--------------|--------|----------|
| **MAOA** | 5'UTR VNTR | 30bp repeats (2R, 3R, 4R, 5R) | 3.5R/4R = ↓ expression | Aggression under stress (context-dependent) |
| **MAOA** | rs6323 | c.891T>C p.Arg297Arg | G allele = ↓ expression | mRNA stability; antidepressant response |
| **MAOA** | rs909525 | Promoter | Transcription | Haplotype with VNTR |
| **MAOA** | rs2064070 | Intronic | Splicing | Depression associations |
| **MAOB** | rs1799836 | Intron 13 | T allele = ↓ activity | Parkinson's protection; dopamine preservation |
| **MAOB** | rs6651806 | 3'UTR | mRNA stability | PD age of onset |

### MAOA VNTR Repeat Effects
| Repeat | Expression Level | Population Frequency | Clinical Notes |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **3R** | Low (~30%) | 35-40% Caucasian | "Warrior gene" association; stress reactivity |
| **4R** | Low-Intermediate (~50%) | 50-55% Caucasian | Reference standard |
| **5R** | High (~100%) | 5-10% Caucasian | Normal/high activity |
| **2R** | Very low | Rare | Similar to 3R |

### Clinical Applications

**Psychiatry:**
- Low MAOA + childhood trauma = ↑ aggression/ASPD risk
- MAO inhibitors (phenelzine, tranylcypromine) more effective with low-expressors
- Antidepressant selection (SSRIs vs. MAOIs)

**Parkinson's Disease:**
- MAOB rs1799836 T allele = slower progression
- MAOB inhibitors (selegiline, rasagiline) more effective
- Dopamine preservation strategy

**Smoking Cessation:**
- MAOA variants affect nicotine metabolism
- MAOB associations with smoking behavior

## **WEEK 18: VDR — Vitamin D Receptor Genomics**

**Topics:**
- Vitamin D metabolism pathway review
- VDR FokI (rs2228570) protein length variant

- Haplotype analysis (BAt vs. baT)
- Vitamin D responsiveness by genotype

### Key VDR Variants
| SNP | cDNA/Protein | Mechanism | Clinical Effect |
|-----|--------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **FokI (rs2228570)** | c.2T>C p.Met1Thr | Protein length; F allele 3aa shorter | F/F = ↑ transcriptional activity |
| **BsmI (rs1544410)** | Intron 8 | mRNA stability | B allele = more stable mRNA |
| **TaqI (rs731236)** | c.1056T>C p.Ser352Ser | Translation efficiency | t allele = ↓ translation |
| **ApaI (rs7975232)** | Intron 8 | Splicing regulation | A allele affects intron retention |
| **Cdx2 (rs11568820)** | Promoter | Intestinal transcription factor | G allele = ↑ intestinal VDR |
| **Poly(A)** | 3'UTR | mRNA stability | Short repeats = stable |

### VDR Haplotypes
| Haplotype | BsmI | ApaI | TaqI | Clinical Associations |
|-----------|------|------|------|----------------------|
| **BAt** | B | A | t | Higher VDR activity; better bone density |
| **baT** | b | a | T | Lower VDR activity; osteoporosis risk |
| **B-a-T** | Mixed | — | — | Intermediate |

### Clinical Applications by Genotype

**Bone Health:**
- ff (FokI) + bb (BsmI) = highest osteoporosis risk
- FF + BB = better response to vitamin D supplementation

**Immune Function:**
- FokI F allele = better immune response
- Autoimmune associations with specific haplotypes

**Cancer Risk:**
- Breast, prostate, colon cancer associations
- Vitamin D chemoprevention by genotype

**Response to Supplementation:**
- ff genotype may need 2-3× higher vitamin D doses
- BsmI bb carriers: check 25-OH-D levels more frequently

### Cofactors for Vitamin D Function
| Cofactor | Function | Deficiency Effect |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Magnesium** | CYP27B1 activation | Low Mg = ↓ 1,25-OH-D |
| **Boron** | 24-hydroxylase regulation | ↓ Vitamin D half-life |
| **K2 (MK-7)** | Osteocalcin activation | ↓ Bone mineralization |
| **Zinc** | VDR DNA binding | ↓ VDR transcriptional activity |

**Case Study:**
> A postmenopausal woman with osteoporosis has ff (FokI) and bb (BsmI) genotype. She's been taking 2000 IU vitamin D3 daily but 25-OH-D remains 22 ng/mL. Design an optimized vitamin D protocol.

- [ ] Review vitamin D cofactor interactions

**Clinical Pearl:**
> "VDR FokI is one of the few variants where dosing truly matters—ff homozygotes often need 4000-8000 IU/day to achieve optimal 25-OH-D levels, while FF carriers may do fine with 1000-2000 IU. Always check levels and adjust accordingly."

### Clinical Applications
