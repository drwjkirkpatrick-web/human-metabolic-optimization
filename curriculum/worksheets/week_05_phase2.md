## Part 2: Drug-Gene Interactions by Phenotype

**NAT2 Slow Acetylator (50% of Caucasians, >90% of some Asian populations):**
| Drug | Action Required |
|------|-----------------|
| Isoniazid | **High risk of peripheral neuropathy** — add B6 prophylaxis |
| Hydralazine | **Lupus-like syndrome risk** — monitor ANA |
| Procainamide | **SLE risk** — consider alternatives |
| Sulfonamides | **Hypersensitivity / Stevens-Johnson risk** |
| Dapsone | **Methemoglobinemia risk** |

**UGT1A1 *28/*28 (Poor glucuronidation):**
| Drug | Action Required |
|------|-----------------|
| Irinotecan | **Severe neutropenia / diarrhea** — dose reduce or avoid |
| Atazanavir | **Hyperbilirubinemia** — generally benign |
| Nilotinib | **Monitor liver function** |

**COMT Met/Met (Low activity = "Worrier"):**
| Outcome | Risk |
|---------|------|
| Dopamine persistence | Higher peak stress response, anxiety risk |
| Estrogen clearance | Relative estrogen dominance |
| Pain sensitivity | **Higher pain scores** — opioid response may be blunted |
| Cognitive style | Better sustained attention, worse cognitive flexibility |

**COMT Val/Val (High activity = "Warrior"):**
| Outcome | Risk |
|---------|------|
| Dopamine rapid clearance | More resilient under stress, lower anxiety |
| Pain sensitivity | **Lower pain tolerance** — may need higher opioid dosing |
| Cognitive style | Better cognitive flexibility under stress |

## Part 3: Cofactor Support for Phase II

**Acetylation (NAT2):**
- [ ] **Vitamin C** — supports N-hydroxylation before acetylation
- [ ] **Molybdenum** — cofactor for sulfite oxidase (parallel detox)
- [ ] **B5 (Pantothenic acid)** — acetyl-CoA synthesis (substrate for N-acetylation)

**Glucuronidation (UGT):**
- [ ] **Magnesium** — UGT is a Mg²⁺-dependent enzyme
- [ ] **Cruciferous vegetables** — indole-3-carbinol induces UGT1A1
- [ ] **Calcium D-glucarate** — inhibits beta-glucuronidase (preserves glucuronides)

**Methylation (COMT):**
- [ ] **Magnesium** — required for COMT catalytic activity
- [ ] **SAMe** — methyl donor (source: methionine cycle)
- [ ] **B12 + folate** — maintain methionine → SAMe flux

## Clinical Pearl
> The "acetylator paradox": Some drugs (isoniazid, sulfasalazine) are prodrugs activated by NAT2. Slow acetylators get MORE parent drug toxicity but LESS active metabolite efficacy. For sulfasalazine in inflammatory bowel disease, rapid acetylators actually have better clinical response. Always know whether the drug is an active compound or a prodrug before counseling a NAT2 patient.
