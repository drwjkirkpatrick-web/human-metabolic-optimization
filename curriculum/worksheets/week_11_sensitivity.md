## Part 2: Herxheimer Mechanism and Prevention

**What is a Herxheimer reaction?**
An acute inflammatory response triggered by the rapid release of endotoxins (LPS, peptidoglycans), cytokines, or mobilized toxins exceeding the emunctories' clearance capacity.

**Biochemical rationale in SNP carriers:**
| Pathway bottleneck | Resulting toxin accumulation |
|-------------------|------------------------------|
| GSTM1/GSTT1 Null | Environmental chemical intermediates (reactive quinones) |
| NAT2 Slow | Acetylated drug/toxin intermediates (hydroxylamines) |
| CYP2D6 PM | Parent drug accumulation (codeine, tamoxifen) |
| MTHFR C677T | Homocysteine, SAH (methylation backlog) |
| Low albumin | Unbound circulating toxins (reduced protein binding) |

## Part 3: Pre-Mobilization Priming Protocol

**Mandatory for all High/Very High risk patients:**

**Phase A: GST Priming (2-4 weeks)**
- [ ] NAC 300–600 mg/day → cysteine substrate for glutathione
- [ ] Selenium 100–200 mcg/day → GPx and GST peroxidase cofactor
- [ ] Vitamin C 500–1000 mg/day → reduces GSSG back to GSH
- [ ] Alpha-lipoic acid 100–300 mg/day → recycles glutathione, chelates metals
- [ ] Glycine 1–2 g/day (if tolerated) → rate-limiting amino acid for GSH synthesis
- [ ] Cruciferous vegetables 1–2 cups/day → sulforaphane induces remaining GST isoforms

**Phase B: Bile Flow Support (1–2 weeks)**
- [ ] Taurine 500 mg/day → bile acid conjugation
- [ ] Phosphatidylcholine 1–2 g/day → membrane fluidity, bile flow
- [ ] Bitter herbs (gentian, dandelion, artichoke) → CCK stimulation, choleresis
- [ ] Hydration 2–3 L/day → maintains bile & urine output

**Phase C: Emunctorial Drainage (ongoing)**
- [ ] Hydration (minimum 2 L/day, more with mobilization)
- [ ] Fiber 25–35 g/day → enterohepatic recirculation interruption
- [ ] Dry skin brushing / sauna (low & slow) → skin emunctory support
- [ ] Breathing exercises / nasal irrigation → pulmonary emunctory
- [ ] Kidney support (Nettle, Dandelion leaf) → renal emunctory

## Part 4: Acute Herxheimer Management

**If Herxheimer develops during mobilization:**

| Symptom severity | Action |
|-------------------|--------|
| Mild (fatigue, mild headache, achiness) | **Reduce mobilizing agent dose 50%**; increase binders |
| Moderate (fever, chills, significant malaise) | **STOP mobilizing agent**; binders every 2–4 hours; hydration aggressively |
| Severe (confusion, chest pain, hypotension, anaphylaxis signs) | **STOP all agents**; urgent medical evaluation; rule out sepsis |

**Acute binder protocol:**
| Toxin source | Preferred binder |
|-------------|------------------|
| Heavy metals (Hg, Pb, Cd, As) | Chlorella + humic/fulvic acids + silica |
| Mycotoxins (mold) | Activated charcoal + bentonite clay + S. boulardii |
| Endotoxins (LPS) | Therapeutic clay + fiber + S. boulardii |
| Environmental chemicals | Charcoal + chlorella + fiber |

**Never use IV chelation in acute Herxheimer.** It removes the offending agent (mobilizer) but leaves the patient in rebound toxin surge with no compensatory capacity.

## Clinical Pearl
> The "three-prime rule" for chemical-sensitive patients: Prime the GST buffers, prime the bile flow, prime the elimination pathways — in that order, for a minimum of 2-4 weeks, before ever touching a mobilizing agent. Dr. William Rea (environmental medicine pioneer) observed that the single factor determining whether mold/CIRS patients could tolerate building remediation was pre-treatment emunctory priming. Skip this step and you create iatrogenic illness from well-intentioned detox.
