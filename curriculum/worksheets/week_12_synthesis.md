## Answer Key (for instructor)

**Primary bottlenecks:**
1. **Compound heterozygous MTHFR** (C677T + A1298C) + MTR/MTRR variants = severe methylation bottleneck
2. **GSTM1 Null** = Phase II deficiency; cannot handle endogenous byproducts or environmental toxins
3. **CYP2C19 IM** = impaired omeprazole/citalopram clearance (potential accumulation)

**Medication interactions:**
- Citalopram: metabolized by CYP2C19 (major) + CYP2D6 (minor). At 20 mg in a CYP2C19 IM, level may be 30-50% higher. Consider dose reduction to 10 mg or switching to escitalopram.
- Omeprazole: CYP2C19 substrate. IM metabolizes slowly → prolonged acid suppression → B12/magnesium malabsorption → WORSENS the methylation picture. **Switch to H2 blocker or PPI not dependent on 2C19** (lansoprazole less affected).
- Oral contraceptive: estrogen component metabolized by CYP3A4. CYP3A5 non-expressor doesn't significantly alter ethinyl estradiol. However, oral estrogen PLUS COMT Val/Met (intermediate clearance) = estrogen dominance risk. Consider IUD or progesterone-only.

**Protocol design:**
- 5-MTHF 400-800 mcg/day (NOT folic acid — MTHFR cannot reduce folic acid efficiently)
- Methylcobalamin 1000 mcg/day (NOT cyano — requires decyanation, uses glutathione which is scarce in GSTM1 Null)
- B6 25-50 mg P5P
- Riboflavin 10-25 mg (MTHFR flavin cofactor)
- Mg 400 mg glycinate or malate (cofactor for MTR/MTRR, COMT, ATP reactions)
- NAC 600 mg/day (supports glutathione in GSTM1 Null)
- Selenium 200 mcg/day
- Vitamin C 1000 mg/day
- Zinc 25-30 mg elemental (picolinate or glycinate). Do NOT give supplemental copper — patient is already low-normal but not deficient. Oversupply can worsen methylation by competing with zinc-dependent enzymes.

**COMT considerations:**
- COMT Val/Met + estrogen + SSRI = potential for excessive serotonin if methyl donors added. 
- If adding SAMe, start VERY low (100 mg) and monitor for anxiety/insomnia.

**Ultimate recommendation:** Consider removing oral contraceptive (estrogen load on already impaired COMT) unless strong contraceptive need. An IUD or barrier method removes this burden.
