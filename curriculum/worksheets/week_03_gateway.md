## Part 2: Clinical Consequences of Altered P-gp

**Low P-gp function** → drugs stay in systemic circulation longer:
| Drug Class | Specific Risk |
|-----------|---------------|
| Digoxin | Toxicity (↑ levels) |
| Cyclosporine | Nephrotoxicity |
| Tacrolimus | Neurotoxicity / nephrotoxicity |
| Fexofenadine | ↑ efficacy but ↑ CNS sedation |
| Docetaxel / paclitaxel | Myelosuppression |

**High P-gp function** → subtherapeutic levels:
| Drug Class | Specific Risk |
|-----------|---------------|
| Loperamide | Inefficacy for diarrhea |
| Antiretrovirals | Virological failure |
| Antiepileptics | Breakthrough seizures |

---

## Part 3: Interventions Based on Phenotype

**For Low P-gp (accumulators):**
- [ ] **Grapefruit** → *Avoid* (CYP3A4 + P-gp inhibition)
- [ ] **St. John's Wort** → *Avoid* (CYP3A4 + P-gp induction paradox)
- [ ] **Ketoconazole** → Monitor levels strictly
- [ ] **Dose reduction** of substrate drugs by 25-50%
- [ ] **Berberine** → Natural P-gp modulation (use cautiously)

**For High P-gp (rapid clearers):**
- [ ] **Piperine (black pepper)** → transient P-gp inhibition
- [ ] **Quercetin** → flavonoid-based modulation
- [ ] **Consider dose escalation** under monitoring
- [ ] **Extended-release formulations** may help sustain levels

---

## Clinical Pearl
> A patient on tacrolimus with MDR1 3435TT genotype has approximately 30% higher trough levels than CC genotype at the same dose. Dose reduction and more frequent monitoring are mandatory. This is especially critical in solid organ transplant recipients.
