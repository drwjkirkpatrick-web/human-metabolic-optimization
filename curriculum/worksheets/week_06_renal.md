## Part 2: Clinical Consequences

**ACE DD (High angiotensin II, high ACE activity):**
| Outcome | Clinical Management |
|---------|---------------------|
| Hypertension | **Excellent response to ACE inhibitors** |
| Salt sensitivity | >10 mmHg BP drop on low-salt diet |
| Left ventricular mass | Higher risk of hypertrophy |
| Athletic performance | **Endurance advantage** (less fatigue, better VO2 max) |
| COVID-19 severity | Controversial data — DD may confer higher risk |

**ACE II (Low ACE activity):**
| Outcome | Clinical Management |
|---------|---------------------|
| Hypertension | **Poor response to ACE inhibitors** — use ARBs or CCBs |
| Salt sensitivity | Minimal BP response to salt restriction |
| Athletic performance | **Strength advantage** (higher muscle hypertrophy response) |
| Inflammatory response | Higher bradykinin → cough with ACE inhibitors |

**SLC2A9 risk variants (rs16890979 T allele):**
| Outcome | Management |
|---------|-----------|
| Urate clearance ↓ | Higher gout risk |
| Fructose sensitivity | Fructose load ↑ urate |
| Allopurinol response | May need higher dosing |

## Part 3: Nutritional and Lifestyle Interventions

**For ACE DD (renin-angiotensin overactive):**
- [ ] **Low-sodium diet** → synergizes with ACEi
- [ ] **Beetroot / nitrate** → NO pathway bypass
- [ ] **Hibiscus tea** → mild ACE inhibition
- [ ] **Vitamin D** → modulates RAAS activity
- [ ] **Omega-3** → reduces angiotensin II sensitivity

**For ACE II (low ACE, bradykinin-dominant):**
- [ ] **Consider ARB** instead of ACEi (no bradykinin cough)
- [ ] **Protein intake** — moderate; excessive may be poorly tolerated
- [ ] **Hydration** — maintain adequate volume

**For gout risk (SLC2A9 / ABCG2):**
- [ ] **Avoid / limit:** Beer, shellfish, organ meats, high-fructose corn syrup
- [ ] **Promote:** Cherries, coffee, vitamin C, dairy (low-fat)
- [ ] **Consider:** Allopurinol or febuxostat if aprematurent

---

## Clinical Pearl
> ACE I/D polymorphism explains why some patients get the "ACE inhibitor cough" and hypertension control while others don't. II genotype patients clear bradykinin poorly → cough. DD genotype patients have high ACE → good response to ACEi. This is a classic example of why pharmacogenomic testing BEFORE starting an ACEi can predict both efficacy and adverse effects.
